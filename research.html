Moral and Political Psychology Research

Shayan Syed is a PhD student in Psychology at Lakehead University in Thunder Bay, Ontario. His research examines how moral values, political identity, and social information shape judgments about other people and decisions about social affiliation.

Current research focus

Political identities can operate as cues people use when deciding whom to trust, befriend, collaborate with, or avoid. Shayan's doctoral research investigates whether people rely more on shared political labels, shared moral values, or both when evaluating potential social partners.

This work draws on moral psychology, political psychology, social cognition, coalition-based accounts of group behaviour, and research on interpersonal affiliation. It examines how people interpret social information in contexts where political identity and moral values may align, conflict, or be absent.

Research interests

Moral psychology

How moral values guide social judgment, political attitudes, and perceptions of similarity or difference.

Political psychology

Political identity, ideological coalitions, intergroup perception, and the psychological meaning of political labels.

Social affiliation

How people select friends, collaborators, and other social partners when they have limited information.

Research methods

Research design, replication, survey methodology, data quality, quantitative analysis, and transparent research practice.

AI in education

How artificial intelligence affects learning, cognitive offloading, academic integrity, and research practice.

Helping and prosocial behaviour

Individual and situational factors that influence helping, cooperation, and social support.
